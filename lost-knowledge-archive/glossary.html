Reference

Glossary

Short definitions for recurring terms used across Corpus Nexus, from forbidden texts and sacred geometry to UAP, reset theory, and spiritual practice.

Status: Site reference Reliability: Working definitions Section: Reference

Core Terms

Term Working Meaning
ApocryphaReligious writings outside a particular community's accepted canon, though some traditions may preserve them as scripture or sacred history.
CanonThe accepted collection of sacred texts recognized by a religious community.
DisclosureThe public release or claimed revelation of hidden information, often about UAP, secret programs, ancient knowledge, or classified technology.
EsotericInner, hidden, or specialized teaching intended for initiated, trained, or prepared students.
ExotericOuter or public teaching available to the general community.
FirmamentA biblical and ancient cosmological term for the heavens or sky structure, interpreted differently across traditions.
GnosisSpiritual knowledge or direct insight, especially in traditions that distinguish inner knowing from ordinary belief.
HermeticRelating to teachings attributed to Hermes Trismegistus, often involving correspondence, alchemy, astrology, and hidden laws of nature.
Mud FloodA modern hidden-history theory claiming that buried lower floors, raised streets, or sediment layers point to a forgotten catastrophe or reset.
OccultHidden or concealed knowledge; not automatically evil, though modern usage often gives it a dark tone.
RefractionBending of light through atmosphere or different media, important in long-distance visibility and curvature debates.
Sacred GeometrySymbolic and spiritual interpretation of geometric forms, ratios, and patterns as expressions of cosmic order.
TheosisAncient Christian teaching of becoming united with God by grace, being healed and transformed while remaining human.
UAPUnidentified anomalous phenomena; an official term broader than UFO and not automatically extraterrestrial.

How to Use This Page

Use these as starting definitions. Many terms change meaning depending on tradition, century, language, and source. When a topic becomes important, follow it back to primary texts, official records, or documented historical use.
